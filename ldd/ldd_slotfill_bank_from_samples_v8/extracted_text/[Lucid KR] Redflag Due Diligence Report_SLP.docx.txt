KEY STRUCTURAL FINDINGS

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
This section analyzes the fundamental changes to the corporate, governance, and intellectual property structure of Lucid KR as a direct result of the Series A financing and the concurrent corporate restructuring (collectively, the “Transaction”). A key outcome of the Transaction, effectuated through a share exchange, is the consolidation of the group structure whereby Lucid KR will become a wholly-owned subsidiary of Lucid SG. We compare the Company’s structure prior to the Transaction with the confirmed consolidated structure to be implemented upon closing.

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
Existing Structure

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
Prior to the Transaction, Lucid KR operated as a standalone Korean corporation with a relatively simple structure:

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
[Corporate & Capital Structure]: The Company was established as a private stock company (주식회사) under the laws of the Republic of Korea on January 26, 2023. As of December 24, 2024, its capitalization consisted of 120,000 issued shares with a par value of KRW 100 per share. This was the result of a December 28, 2023 shareholder resolution that increased the authorized share capital from 100,000 to 10,000,000 shares and authorized the issuance of 20,000 Convertible Preferred Shares to its first institutional investor, First Gate Private Investment Partnership No. 5 (“First Gate”), for an investment of KRW 500,000,000. The shareholding structure was as follows:

[Governance]: Corporate governance was centered around the founder, Kisun Lee, who has been the sole internal director (사내이사) and sole representative director (단독대표) since the Company’s inception. Key investor protections, such as consent rights on major corporate actions, tag-along rights, and put options upon default, were held exclusively by First Gate pursuant to its investment agreement. There was no contractual or corporate mechanism in place for another outside investor, such as the Client, to exercise control over the board or key corporate decisions.

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
[Intellectual Property]: The foundational IP ownership model was already established prior to the Transaction, governed by the Research and Development Services Agreement between Lucid KR and its Singaporean affiliate, Lucid SG. This agreement contractually defines Lucid KR as the “Service Provider” and stipulates that all intellectual property developed by Lucid KR is legally owned by Lucid SG. While this contractual arrangement existed, the ownership and governance of the two entities were not consolidated, and Lucid KR operated as a distinct Korean legal entity.

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
Post-Transaction Structure

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
The Transaction fundamentally transforms the group’s structure by contractually and corporately consolidating the entities under Lucid SG, with the Client as a lead investor.

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
Corporate & Capital Structure: Post-transaction, Lucid KR will become a wholly-owned subsidiary of Lucid SG. This is achieved via a multi-step process governed by the master Financing Agreement:

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
Dual-Track Investment: The Client will purchase Series A Redeemable Convertible Preferred Stock directly in Lucid SG under a Stock Purchase Agreement. Concurrently, Korean investors like Vision Equity Partners will purchase Redeemable Convertible Preferred Stock (RCPS) in Lucid KR, and existing SAFE notes will convert into RCPS of Lucid KR.

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
In-Kind Contribution (The “Swap”): All shareholders of Lucid KR (including the founder, First Gate, and new Korean investors) are contractually obligated to contribute their Lucid KR shares to Lucid SG. In exchange, they will receive new shares of Lucid SG (e.g., Series A-1 Preferred Stock). This structure is necessary as a direct “comprehensive share exchange” is not available for a foreign parent under the Korean Commercial Code.

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
Governance: The new structure institutes a multi-layered governance framework designed to give the Client effective oversight:

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
Direct Board Right at Lucid SG: The Client, as a holder of Series A Preferred Stock, will have the direct right to appoint a director to the board of the parent company, Lucid SG, as stipulated in Lucid SG’s amended Constitution.

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
Indirect Board Right at Lucid KR: The Client secures a board seat at Lucid KR through a “back-to-back” contractual arrangement.

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
the Korean Shareholders’ Agreement (SHA), signed by all Lucid KR shareholders, grants the founder (Kisun Lee) the right to appoint two of the three directors.

[BASE] Scope and Methodology
the master Financing Agreement imposes a binding contractual obligation on the founder to use one of his two nomination rights to appoint a director designated by the Client. This obligation is enforced by a penalty clause within the Financing Agreement, granting the Client a call option to acquire the founder’s shares at par value upon a breach.

[H3] Legal Analysis of the New Structure
[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
From the Client’s perspective, the new structure introduces significant advantages over the existing one, but also carries inherent complexity and risks.

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Advantages

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Establishes Control and Oversight: The primary advantage is the establishment of robust governance rights where none existed for the Client previously. The new structure provides the Client with board representation at both the top-level holding company (Lucid SG) and the key operational entity (Lucid KR), allowing for direct oversight of its investment and the Company’s strategic direction.

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Aligns Economic Interests: By making Lucid KR a wholly-owned subsidiary of Lucid SG via the share swap mechanism detailed in the Financing Agreement, the economic interests of all stakeholders (including founders, new, and existing investors) are consolidated and aligned under a single parent entity. This eliminates potential conflicts that could arise from operating two separate but interconnected companies.

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Centralized IP Management: The structure formally centralizes all core IP under Lucid SG, as defined in the R&D Services Agreement. This can streamline future IP licensing, global enforcement against third parties, and strategic partnerships, creating a cleaner structure for a potential future exit.

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Risks

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Complexity of Enforcement: The key risk lies in the complexity of the governance structure. The Client’s board seat at Lucid KR is indirect and relies on enforcing a contractual obligation against the founder under the Financing Agreement, rather than a direct right in the Company’s charter. While designed for enforceability with a penalty at par value, this remedy is inherently more cumbersome to execute than a direct appointment right.

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Execution Risk: The entire structure is contingent upon the successful and simultaneous execution of multiple, interdependent agreements and corporate actions, including the SG-level Stock Purchase Agreement, multiple KR-level Investment Agreements, the SAFE conversions, the comprehensive Share Swap, and the founder’s share transfer. A failure at any single step could jeopardize the entire transaction.

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
External Dependencies: The viability of the new structure is subject to material external approvals, which were not a factor for the simpler existing structure. These include obtaining explicit consents from Hana Bank and KODIT regarding the change of control under the KRW 1 billion loan and receiving confirmation from the TIPS program administrator that the new foreign-owned subsidiary structure does not trigger penalties. These dependencies represent significant closing risks.

Indebtedness & Financing Agreements

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Finding: Existing KRW 1 Billion Loan and Change of Control Risk

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
The Company has an outstanding KRW 1,000,000,000 credit facility, maturing on January 22, 2026, under a “Credit Transaction Agreement” (여신거래약정서) with Hana Bank. This loan is supported by a “Credit Guarantee Agreement” (신용보증약정서) from the Korea Credit Guarantee Fund (KODIT), which guarantees 90% of the principal, amounting to KRW 900,000,000.

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Pursuant to Article 14, Paragraph 2 of the Credit Transaction Agreement (CTA), the Company is obligated to consult with the Bank in advance of any event that could reasonably be expected to cause a material change in its business or management.

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Implication: Standard loan and guarantee agreements in Korea contain covenants that restrict significant changes in corporate control or major shareholding. The proposed transaction, which will result in Lucid KR becoming a wholly-owned subsidiary of a foreign entity (Lucid SG), will almost certainly trigger these “change of control” provisions. A breach of these covenants could constitute an event of default, allowing the lender to accelerate the debt and demand immediate repayment of the entire outstanding principal and interest.

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Risk Level: Medium

[IP] Intellectual Property > Legal Analysis of the New Structure
Recommendation: It is a critical condition precedent to closing that the Company obtains explicit, prior written consent for the transaction from both Hana Bank and the Korea Credit Guarantee Fund (KODIT).

[H2] Regulatory & Government Grants
[H2] Finding: Conditions of Government R&D Grant (TIPS Program)
[IP] Finding: Conditions of Government R&D Grant (TIPS Program)
The Company is a beneficiary of the Korean government’s “Deep Tech TIPS (R&D)” program, administered by the Ministry of SMEs and Startups (중소벤처기업부) and the Korea Technology and Information Promotion Agency for SMEs (TIPA, 중소기업기술정보진흥원).

[IP] Finding: Conditions of Government R&D Grant (TIPS Program)
The specific project (No. RS-2024-00441512) is titled “High-efficiency, high-power-density, high-integration GaN power semiconductor IC technology development” and runs for 36 months from July 2024 to June 2027.

[IP] Finding: Conditions of Government R&D Grant (TIPS Program)
The total government funding is KRW 1.5 billion, with the Company committed to a matching contribution of KRW 450.1 million in cash and KRW 50.4 million in-kind.

[IP] Finding: Conditions of Government R&D Grant (TIPS Program)
Implication: The TIPS program is designed to support domestic Small & Medium Enterprises (SMEs). A change in the Company’s status to a foreign-owned subsidiary could violate the program’s eligibility criteria. Per the grant agreement, this could trigger penalties or termination under Articles 8 and 22, potentially leading to the termination of future funding and a demand for repayment (“clawback”) of funds already disbursed.

[IP] Finding: Conditions of Government R&D Grant (TIPS Program)
Risk Level: Medium

[IP] Finding: Conditions of Government R&D Grant (TIPS Program)
Recommendation: The Client must require, as a condition to closing, that the Company obtain a definitive written confirmation from the program administrator (TIPA) stating that the change of control will not trigger any penalties or repayment obligations under the TIPS agreement.

[H2] Intellectual Property
[IP] Intellectual Property
Finding #1: Joint Ownership of IP with Seoul National University

[IP] Intellectual Property
Finding: The Company has an R&D contract with the Seoul National University Industry-Academic Cooperation Foundation (서울대학교 산학협력단), led by Professor Shin, Jong-hoon, for the development of a “65W charging circuit,” valued at KRW 50,000,000.

[IP] Intellectual Property
Article 5 of this contract explicitly stipulates that any intellectual property resulting from this research is jointly owned by the Company and the university. For the Company to use this IP exclusively, it is required to pay royalties.

[IP] Intellectual Property
Implication: This is a significant encumbrance. The jointly-owned IP cannot be freely used or transferred to Lucid SG as part of the group’s consolidated IP portfolio without the consent of a third party (the university). This directly conflicts with the core IP strategy of the Lucid group.

[IP] Intellectual Property
Risk Level: Low

[IP] Intellectual Property
Recommendation: The commercial importance of this jointly-owned IP must be assessed. If it is deemed critical, the Client should require the Company to negotiate a full assignment of the IP from the university prior to closing. Alternatively, a perpetual, royalty-free, worldwide exclusive license with the right to sublicense to Lucid SG must be secured.

[H2] Finding #2: Risk of Employee Invention Claims
[LITIGATION] Finding #2: Risk of Employee Invention Claims
Finding: The Company’s standard Employment Agreement (in Article 8, “Intellectual Property Rights”) contains a clause assigning rights to all employee inventions to the Company.

[LITIGATION] Finding #2: Risk of Employee Invention Claims
However, as advised by Korean counsel, under Korea’s Invention Promotion Act, such an assignment may be challenged if “reasonable compensation,” separate from standard salary, is not provided to the employee for the invention. The reviewed employment and salary agreements do not specify any such separate compensation scheme.

[LITIGATION] Finding #2: Risk of Employee Invention Claims
Implication: This creates a latent risk that former or current employees could later assert claims for additional compensation or, in a worst-case scenario, challenge the Company’s ownership of key IP. This poses a potential obstacle to the clean transfer and consolidation of IP under Lucid SG.

[LITIGATION] Finding #2: Risk of Employee Invention Claims
Risk Level: Low

[LITIGATION] Finding #2: Risk of Employee Invention Claims
Recommendation: The Company should conduct an audit of its key IP assets to identify the inventing employees and assess the potential liability. Implementing a formal Employee Invention Compensation Policy is recommended to mitigate future risks.
